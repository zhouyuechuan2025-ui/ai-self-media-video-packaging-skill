# Director and evidence rules

## Semantic direction

- Establish one readable tension, question, or result in the first three seconds.
- Change structure when the reasoning changes: claim, proof, loop, command, sequence, comparison, evidence, metric, route, or metaphor.
- Do not animate every noun. One visual state should carry one meaningful relationship.
- Avoid more than two consecutive instances of the same structure. Alternate ordinary left/right lanes.
- A video longer than 30 seconds needs at least eight valid structures; diversity must come from the speech, not random assignment.

## Center-presenter protection

- Protect center x=35%–65% and bottom 18% by default.
- Protect faces, products, source UI, nameplates, and burned-in captions after inspecting actual frames.
- A full-screen beat must preserve a presenter window or intentionally dim the source; it must not accidentally cover the person with dense copy.

## Facts and evidence

- A spoken number remains a speaker claim unless independently verified.
- Mark numbers as `sourced`, `owner-confirmed`, or `estimate`; never silently promote one status to another.
- `evidence-panel` requires an approved local screenshot/document, a source label, and a restrained interpretation.
- If the source asset is absent, downgrade to `thesis-and-proof`; do not fabricate a proof card.
- A doodle explains action or transformation. It never functions as evidence.

## Source continuity

- Preserve the source edit and audio by default.
- A browser-compatible H.264/AAC proxy may be generated without cuts when the source codec cannot be decoded in the renderer.
- If captions are already burned in, do not add another caption track.
